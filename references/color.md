# Color

Color is a system of roles, contrast relationships, and attention budgets. Derive it from brand evidence, subject matter, state meaning, and the environment where the interface is used. Do not add an accent only to make a neutral interface feel alive.

## Establish roles first

Define semantic roles before choosing decorative swatches:

- page background;
- surface and elevated surface;
- primary and secondary text;
- border and divider;
- accent or brand action;
- focus and selection;
- success, warning, danger, and informational states;
- disabled and unavailable content.

Each role needs a job, a contrast relationship, and a behavior across hover, active, focus, disabled, dark, and light contexts when those modes exist. A palette is not a system until components consume roles consistently.

## Derive, then test

Start with the material that already belongs to the product: brand assets, source imagery, domain conventions, content semantics, or operational state. If no evidence exists, choose a restrained starting palette and let the hierarchy, not the number of colors, create emphasis.

Test actual text, controls, charts, disabled states, and overlays. Do not use opacity to make important information disappear. Do not assume a color is meaningful because it is saturated; a color must be learnable and distinguishable in context.

Reserve the strongest contrast and most saturated values for the actions or data that deserve attention. If every section has a high-salience accent, there is no priority.

## Surface treatment

Flat color, texture, image, gradient, transparency, and shadow are all valid when they explain a layer, material, state, or brand behavior. Ask what the treatment communicates:

- a gradient may express a known transition, light source, data field, or brand asset;
- transparency may establish spatial continuity over changing content;
- a border may show a boundary or state;
- elevation may show ownership or interaction order;
- texture may carry subject matter or improve wayfinding.

Remove treatments that only fill empty space or make a generic composition look finished.

## Theme parity

If the product supports multiple themes, preserve semantic hierarchy rather than copying hex values. Re-evaluate contrast, focus visibility, elevation, image treatment, chart meaning, and disabled states in each theme. Dark mode is not a simple color inversion.

## Why test

For every strong color decision, finish: "I chose this role and contrast because this product needs users to distinguish X, prioritize Y, or recognize Z in this context." If the answer is merely mood, find the semantic or brand evidence that gives the color a job.
